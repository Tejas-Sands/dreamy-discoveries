# What to make next

Research date: **12 September 2026**. Planning window: **13 September–31 December 2026**. Confirmed audience: global English-speaking families, with some India-specific dates. The plan retains ages 2–5 as its working age range.

**Recommendation: build repeatable preschool learning series, add useful routine/feelings stories, publish seasonal episodes before the event, and retain a weekly flexible slot.** The evidence supports these content families. It does not establish that any individual title will go viral. This is a research-backed editorial calendar, not a forecast of views.

## What the research actually shows

| Finding | Evidence and its limits | Decision for this channel |
|---|---|---|
| Preschool music remains a substantial, current category | YouTube's 27 August 2026 profile reports Skyship/Super Simple's large audience and ongoing use for language, movement, and emotional learning. That is established demand, not evidence that a new channel can reproduce its reach. [YouTube profile](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/super-simple-songs-20th-anniversary-youtube/) | Start with counting, colors, shapes, opposites, and movement. Establish one baseline per format before multiplying variants. |
| Routines answer concrete parent needs | Sesame Workshop recommends breaking everyday routines into manageable steps; Super Simple maintains cleanup, handwashing, and bath resources. These sources establish usefulness and existing supply, not search-volume rankings. [Sesame routines](https://sesameworkshop.org/resources/making-everyday-routines-go-smoothly/), [cleanup collection](https://supersimple.com/content-topic/clean-up/) | Make Ben's cleanup story and Daisy's bedtime story early. A deterministic routines template is a useful later investment. |
| Feelings and friendship fit both audience and cast | Sesame Workshop's activities centre on naming feelings and practising supportive responses. [Feelings resource](https://sesameworkshop.org/resources/talking-about-feelings-3/) | Use small, recognizable problems: waiting, joining play, asking for a break, fixing a mistake, and including a friend. Avoid a lecture followed by a disconnected moral. |
| Movement-plus-listening is worth testing | PBS has a 2026 math-and-movement freeze-dance clip; Super Simple also has a stop-and-go dance format. Neither source proves a present viral spike. [PBS example](https://www.pbs.org/video/brain-break-practice-patterns-silly-dance/), [format example](https://supersimple.com/song/dance-and-stop/) | Prioritize an original freeze-dance template when development resumes. The current `actions` template has movement but is not a freeze game. |
| Halloween and Christmas have established preschool content ecosystems | Super Simple has substantial dedicated Halloween and Christmas teaching collections. [Halloween](https://supersimple.com/content-topic/halloween/), [Christmas](https://supersimple.com/content-topic/christmas/) | Test gentle pumpkin/shadow stories and friendship-focused Christmas stories. Start several weeks ahead; lead times are our editorial choice, not a measured optimal ranking window. |
| Letter learning is a credible expansion, with more QA required | Super Simple has an extensive alphabet/phonics collection. This establishes an existing category, not a low-competition gap. [Phonics collection](https://supersimple.com/phonics-fun/) | Put first letter sounds behind a reviewed deterministic template and pronunciation checks. Do not label a generic learning episode “phonics.” |
| Educational clarity matters to distribution strategy | YouTube identifies useful learning, positive routines, complete narratives, and coherent storytelling as quality signals; it describes misleading educational packaging and confusing mass-produced content as low quality. [Quality principles](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en) | Promise one clear lesson, show it accurately, and make the title/thumbnail match the actual episode. Changing only the hero or seed is not enough to justify a new upload. |

No channel-specific Studio analytics, live Google Trends export, keyword-volume dataset, or competitor view-velocity series was available for this research. I have **not** labelled any query “rising,” “breakout,” “low competition,” or “high volume” without that evidence. Public creator examples show existing supply and category relevance. Dates show timely opportunities. The schedule's priority numbers are editorial ordering, not viral scores.

## Keep the schedule responsive

Every Wednesday, review the next Saturday's candidate and the following two weeks. Keep the next three production days stable once scripts are approved. Dates farther than two weeks out are provisional; review the whole next month on 1 October, 1 November, and 1 December.

1. In YouTube Studio, inspect searches and relevant breakout/recent videos for this audience. Availability varies, and Shorts content gaps do not automatically validate a five-minute video. [Studio Trends documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757?hl=en-SE)
2. Compare Google Trends **YouTube Search** for the actual target countries, using 90-day and five-year views. Compare like terms in the same geography/time range. Trends uses normalized 0–100 interest, not absolute search volume, and low-volume spikes can be noise. [Google Trends methodology](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533?hl=en)
3. Record a dated signal: query, country, time window, source URL, observation, and why our six-character version serves a child. Keep observations separate from interpretations.
4. Use two independent relevant observations before promoting a candidate: for example, a sustained relative-interest rise plus several recent comparable preschool videos outperforming their own channel's usual early views. Compare similar age, duration, and format; lifetime views on a famous channel are not a breakout metric.
5. Check implementation and lead time. Move only the flexible slot; preserve an already approved script under its slug. If the idea is late for the event, carry its reusable lesson forward under an honest evergreen brief. Do not silently run expired seasonal rows.

The “two observations” rule is our conservative operating heuristic, not a YouTube algorithm rule. No qualifying signal means the flex slot stays unconfirmed. Use a distinct unpublished approved evergreen episode, or skip the day. Do not manufacture a trend to fill the calendar.

Review each upload after 48 hours, seven days, and 28 days. Compare seven-day views and retention against the channel's median for similar-length, similar-format episodes. After at least ten comparable releases, a roughly 1.5× seven-day-view result with retention at or above the median is a reasonable **experimental** trigger for one follow-up—not a universal success threshold. Inspect opening drop-offs before expanding reprises. [YouTube retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)

Use actual YouTube video IDs for results. A GitHub Release URL is delivery storage, not a YouTube analytics identifier. The current stats script does not populate retention or usable CTR, so those require Studio review or later authenticated analytics integration. Collect snapshots rather than treating one overwritten lifetime count as growth. This research adds no paid service, recurring AI call, or background monitoring task.
